## PFM Value Promise

PFM helps physical locations turn movement data into decisions. We show who comes, when they come, how they move, and what that means for the next commercial action.

Why the customer should care: it moves the conversation from hardware and counts to the decisions that improve location performance.

## Geolocation As A Cross-Vertical Capability

Geolocation is a horizontal evidence layer for Tool 2. It can support Retail, Shopping centres / real estate, Public locations, QSR, Travel and Smart Buildings through aggregate movement context: footfall, visit frequency, dwell, routes, zones, behavioural catchment, origin-destination flow, before/after review and benchmark context.

Why the customer should care: many physical-location decisions fail when a total count is treated as the full story. Geolocation helps frame where people come from, how they move, when patterns change and what context needs to be validated before acting.

Rules:

- Treat geolocation as aggregate and privacy-reviewed.
- Keep customer data, commercial data and context overlays conditional on approval.
- Document data veracity before turning movement into a proof claim.
- Never present competitor/vendor marketing claims or external academic results as PFM proof.

## Retail KPI Explainers

- Footfall: the number of people entering the store. It tells you how much opportunity reached the door.
- Passers-by: the opportunity outside the store. It helps separate poor location demand from weak store attraction.
- Capture rate: the share of passers-by who enter. It helps evaluate frontage, windows, entrance and local activation.
- Conversion rate: the share of visitors who buy. It shows whether the store turns traffic into customers.
- Average transaction value: the average value of a sale. It shows how valuable converted visits are.
- Store benchmarking: a fair comparison of stores by opportunity and execution. It helps decide where to coach, invest or drive traffic.
- Campaign impact: movement before, during and after activity. It helps explain whether marketing changed physical behaviour.
- Peak hours: when demand is highest. It helps align staffing and service with real visitor demand.

## Shopping Centre / Real Estate Story

A shopping centre or real-estate asset needs more than total visitor counts. Owners, investors, tenants, leasing teams and marketing teams need to understand asset performance, centre benchmarking, entrances, zones, flows, dwell time, campaign impact, tenant value and rent context.

Why the customer should care: PFM helps turn `how many people came?` into `what does this mean for the asset, tenants and next decision?`

Source-backed narrative points:

- Centre performance discussions benefit from benchmarking, trend comparison and tenant-perspective transparency.
- Footfall signatures help describe how a centre is actually used, instead of relying only on catchment or retail-offer assumptions.
- Micro-location and zone patterns can support entrance, corridor, tenant-placement, opening-hours and operations conversations.
- Rent, tenant sales, turnover and cost context must remain conditional on approved customer data.

## Travel Story

Travel locations need to understand how people arrive, move, pause and concentrate across station or terminal catchments, entrances, routes, waiting areas, retail zones and service points.

Why the customer should care: fixed catchment assumptions and passenger totals can hide how the location is actually used. Movement evidence can make catchment, route, zone and peak-period conversations more concrete. This story is source-informed discovery until travel-specific PFM proof is approved.

Source-informed narrative points:

- Station catchments can vary by location; a single radius or average can be too blunt.
- Distance distribution can add context beyond average distance or total counts.
- Entrance, route and zone evidence can support wayfinding, planning, service placement and travel retail exposure conversations.
- Before/after movement review should stay conditional unless the measurement design supports causal claims.

## Quick Service Restaurant Story

Quick service restaurants need to serve demand quickly and consistently across drive-thru lanes, in-store counters, queues, dayparts and local campaigns.

Why the customer should care: aggregate movement and service-point evidence can help frame labour, queue, promotion, format and location-performance questions. This story is source-informed, but still validation-led until QSR-specific product scope and proof are approved.

Source-informed narrative points:

- QSR conversations should ask which format matters: single-lane drive-thru, multi-lane drive-thru, in-store counter, kiosk, mixed format or other service point.
- Peak demand, queue pressure and service-point flow are safer first concepts than guaranteed throughput or ROI.
- Local geolocation context can support site-demand and daypart questions, but does not prove QSR performance on its own.
- AI ordering, smart queue management and ROI claims require product and proof approval before customer use.

## Smart Building Story

Smart buildings need evidence for how shared spaces are used across the day and week.

Why the customer should care: aggregate movement data can support utilisation, service planning, cleaning, amenities and stakeholder reporting. No dedicated smart-building source files were found in this pass, so this story remains assumption-led and requires privacy, source and use-case validation before customer-facing use.

Assumption-led narrative points:

- Occupancy, utilisation pattern, dwell and zone performance are plausible smart-building KPIs, but need dedicated sources.
- Employee tracking, workplace surveillance and individual profiling are out of scope.
- Any building or workplace movement story must be aggregate, privacy-reviewed and approved.

## Public Location Story

Public locations need evidence for how people move, gather and use shared spaces.

Why the customer should care: movement patterns, place vitality, amenity context, behavioural catchment and resilience patterns can support planning, intervention review and stakeholder reporting. PFM-specific public-location proof still needs validation before customer-facing use.

Source-backed narrative points:

- Footfall can support place-vitality and resilience conversations, but it is not the whole social or economic impact story.
- Amenity mix and public-space context can help explain movement patterns.
- Behavioural catchment can challenge fixed boundaries or assumed service areas.
- Before/after intervention review must stay caveated unless measurement design supports causality.
