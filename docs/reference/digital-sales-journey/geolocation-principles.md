# Geolocation Principles

Source basis: `01-analysis/reference-extractions/geolocation-cross-vertical-extraction.md`, `public-location-reference-extraction.md`, `qsr-reference-extraction.md`, `travel-reference-extraction.md`, and `smart-building-reference-extraction.md`.

## Definition

For Tool 2, geolocation means aggregate movement intelligence about how populations use physical places. It can include footfall, visit frequency, dwell time, origin-destination flow, catchment, distance distribution, routes, zones, peak periods, bottlenecks and before/after change patterns.

Footfall is narrower: it counts people or visits at a location. Geolocation is broader: it helps explain where visitors come from, where they go, how long they stay, how patterns change and which contextual factors may explain the movement.

## Core Principles

| Principle | Use in Tool 2 | Evidence level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Movement needs context. | Pair counts with time, catchment, route, zone, event, weather, amenity, competitive or approved commercial context. | source-backed |
| Behavioural catchment is not the same as a fixed radius. | Ask where visitors actually come from before relying on a generic buffer or isochrone. | source-backed |
| Averages can hide distribution. | Use distance distribution, daypart profile and segment view when a single average is too blunt. | source-backed |
| Before/after is a recurring decision pattern. | Support campaign, layout, intervention, service and recovery reviews with longitudinal movement evidence. | source-backed |
| Veracity matters. | Ask whether the data is representative, stable, comparable, validated and fit for the customer question. | source-backed |
| Geolocation is horizontal. | Reuse the capability across Retail, Shopping centres, Public locations, QSR, Travel and Smart Buildings. | source-backed |
| Claims must stay evidence-levelled. | Separate PFM proof, external research, competitor-market framing and assumptions. | source-backed |

## Capability Boundary

Use geolocation for:

- Aggregate movement explanation.
- Segment, site, catchment and place-use discovery.
- Sales conversations about what to validate next.
- Reviewable local summaries and proof placeholders.

Do not use geolocation in Tool 2 to claim:

- Individual tracking or re-identification.
- Guaranteed ROI, revenue uplift, safety outcome, queue reduction or passenger outcome.
- Approved demographic targeting or personalisation.
- Live integrations or production automation.
- PFM customer proof unless explicitly approved.
