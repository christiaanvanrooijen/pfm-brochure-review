# KPI Library

Source basis: `source-links.md`, reference extractions under `01-analysis/reference-extractions/`, `01-analysis/sales-enablement-input/digital-sales-journey-spreadsheet-extraction.md`, `customer-pains-by-vertical.md`, `03-shared/domain-knowledge/geolocation/geolocation-kpi-map.md`, and `cross-vertical-knowledge/kpis.md`.

| KPI | Simple explanation | Why it matters | Decision it supports | Data needed | What PFM can deliver | Verticals | Example customer question | Example PFM answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Footfall | Number of people entering or visiting a location. | It is the baseline for physical demand and the starting point for conversion, staffing and performance reviews. | Store reviews, marketing impact, opening hours, staffing, centre/venue performance. | People-counting data by time, location and entrance. | People counting, trend dashboards, history and comparison views. | Retail; Shopping centres / real estate; Travel; QSR; Smart buildings; Public locations | Are fewer people coming in, or are sales down for another reason? | We separate visitor demand from what happens after people arrive. |
| Passers-by | Number of people passing near a store, unit or entrance. | It shows outside opportunity before the customer enters. | Storefront, window, entrance, signage and location-quality decisions. | External/passers-by counts near the location, plus store-entry footfall. | Passers-by measurement and opportunity context where technically feasible. | Retail; Travel retail; Shopping centres / real estate | There is traffic outside, but are we attracting enough of it inside? | We compare available traffic with actual entries so you know whether the problem is outside demand or capture. |
| Capture rate | Share of passers-by who enter. | It separates location demand from attraction performance. | Frontage, windows, local campaigns, mall-unit performance, activation planning. | Passers-by plus entry footfall for the same location/time period. | Capture-rate reporting and local-action comparison. | Retail; Shopping centres / real estate; Travel retail | Are our stores capturing enough of the traffic already passing by? | We show the gap between outside opportunity and actual entry, then connect it to local actions. |
| Conversion rate | Share of visitors who buy or complete the defined target action. | It shows whether visitor opportunity becomes commercial value. | Service, staffing, stock, merchandising, queue, promotion and store-execution decisions. | Footfall plus approved transaction or outcome data. | Conversion analysis and dashboards when customer commercial data is available. | Retail; QSR; Travel retail; Shopping centres / real estate | Traffic is healthy, so why are sales still soft? | We look at what happens after people enter and help identify where the opportunity is being lost. |
| Average transaction value | Average value per transaction. | It shows whether successful visits are commercially valuable. | Upsell, cross-sell, basket, assortment, margin and promotion decisions. | Transaction value and transaction count, ideally by store/time period. | Contextual reporting when paired with traffic and conversion. | Retail; QSR; Travel retail | Are we selling enough per customer when they do buy? | We pair average transaction value with traffic and conversion so you see both volume and value. |
| Basket size | Number or value of items in a transaction. | It adds depth to average transaction value and product-affinity conversations. | Bundling, category placement, cross-sell, promotion and training decisions. | POS basket data and product/category data. | Product-ready insight only when customer POS/basket data is supplied and approved. | Retail; QSR | Are shoppers buying the full basket we expected? | We can show this as commercial context next to movement data, but only if the customer provides approved POS data. |
| Revenue per visitor | Revenue generated per visit. | It connects movement to financial performance without reducing the story to sales rank. | Store investment, performance coaching, format comparison and campaign review. | Footfall and sales/revenue by comparable period. | Revenue-per-visitor reporting where sales data is available. | Retail; Travel retail; Shopping centres / real estate | Which store creates the most commercial value from each visit? | We connect traffic and revenue so stores can be compared by opportunity and execution. |
| Store performance benchmarking | Comparable view of stores by opportunity and execution. | It prevents unfair sales-only comparisons between different traffic environments. | Which stores need traffic generation, conversion coaching, investment, relocation or format review. | Footfall, conversion or transaction data, store metadata, format/region context. | Normalised dashboards and store clusters. | Retail; QSR | Which stores are genuinely underperforming? | We compare stores by the opportunity they receive and what they do with it. |
| Sales per square foot | Revenue by sales-floor area. | It links commercial output to space productivity. | Layout, category placement, space planning, real estate and refurbishment decisions. | Sales, floor area and store/category layout data. | Contextual reporting only when floor-area and revenue data are available. | Retail; Shopping centres / real estate | Are we using our selling space well? | We can add movement context to space-productivity decisions, especially where zones or departments are measured. |
| Sales per employee | Revenue by employee or labour input. | It adds labour context, but can mislead without traffic and conversion. | Staffing, training, service model and productivity decisions. | Sales, staffing/labour data and ideally traffic by time period. | Movement-data context for staffing and service reviews. | Retail; QSR; Travel | Are we staffed correctly when demand peaks? | We show real visitor demand by hour so labour decisions are not based only on completed transactions. |
| Peak hours / dayparts | When demand or pressure is highest. | It shows when opportunity, congestion or service risk is greatest. | Staffing, opening hours, service, cleaning, security and campaign timing. | Time-series footfall or occupancy data. | Peak-period dashboards and daypart reporting. | Retail; QSR; Travel; Smart buildings; Public locations; Shopping centres / real estate | When do we need more people or more service capacity? | We show demand by time, not just total daily or weekly volume. |
| Dwell time | How long people stay in a location or zone. | It can indicate engagement, friction, congestion or destination appeal. | Layout, tenant mix, event, comfort, queue, service and experience decisions. | Approved presence or movement data over time. | Dwell-time and zone insight where technically feasible and privacy-approved. | Retail; Shopping centres / real estate; Travel; Smart buildings; Public locations | Are people engaging with the space or just passing through? | We help distinguish useful engagement from friction or congestion. |
| Zone performance | How different areas perform against traffic, dwell, flow or commercial context. | Total footfall can hide weak and strong spaces. | Department layout, signage, tenant placement, activation and space planning. | Zone-level counts, floor plans, location metadata and time periods. | Zone counting, movement maps and dashboards. | Retail; Shopping centres / real estate; Travel; Smart buildings; Public locations | Which departments, corridors or zones need attention? | We make performance visible below the total-count level. |
| Occupancy | How busy a space is at a point in time. | It supports comfort, safety, service and capacity planning. | Staffing, queue management, cleaning, security, capacity and operations. | Live or interval counts by area. | Occupancy insight and reporting where approved. | Shopping centres / real estate; Travel; QSR; Smart buildings; Public locations | When is this area under pressure? | We show when and where the space is busy so operations can respond. |
| Visitor flow | How people move between entrances, zones, floors or spaces. | It turns static counts into journey understanding. | Wayfinding, layout, tenant placement, queue, service and operational planning. | Counts or movement signals across defined areas. | Visitor-flow and zone movement reporting. | Shopping centres / real estate; Travel; Smart buildings; Public locations; Retail | Where do people go after they enter? | We show movement patterns so teams can improve the journey, not only the entrance count. |
| Campaign impact | What changed before, during and after a campaign, event or local action. | It helps prove whether marketing activity affected physical behaviour and commercial outcomes. | Campaign spend, promotion planning, event review and local activation. | Time-based traffic, campaign calendar and approved sales/transaction context where available. | Before/during/after movement reporting and summary dashboards. | Retail; Shopping centres / real estate; QSR; Travel; Public locations | Did this campaign increase store visits or better conversion? | We separate visit impact from conversion impact so the team knows what changed. |
| Entrance counts | How people enter a centre, store, venue or building. | It reveals strong and weak arrival points that total footfall can hide. | Access, signage, parking, transport, tenant exposure, operations and wayfinding. | Entrance-level counts and defined entrance metadata. | Entrance-level counting, trend views and movement summaries. | Shopping centres / real estate; Retail; Travel; Public locations | Which entrances are working, and which are being missed? | We show the arrival pattern before the team decides on signage, access or activation. |
| Indexing | Comparing movement against history, target, market context or another approved benchmark. | It helps explain whether performance change is isolated or part of a wider pattern. | Owner, investor, asset-management, marketing and operations reviews. | Approved historical data, peer context or market index source. | Indexed trend views when relevant comparison data is approved. | Shopping centres / real estate; Retail; Public locations | Is our performance really down, or is the whole market softer? | We put the movement trend in context so the discussion does not stop at a raw count. |
| Centre benchmarking | Comparing centre performance with relevant context, history or peer views. | It supports a more transparent asset-performance story for owners, managers, tenants and leasing teams. | Asset reporting, tenant mix, leasing, marketing, rent context and management review. | Footfall history, centre metadata, approved peer or market context and stakeholder definitions. | Benchmarking dashboards and stakeholder-ready summaries where comparison data is approved. | Shopping centres / real estate | Where does this centre stand, and what should management review next? | We turn visitor movement into a centre-performance conversation rather than a standalone count. |
| Footfall signatures | Recurring temporal patterns in how a centre or location is used. | It helps classify actual usage patterns instead of relying only on catchment or tenant mix assumptions. | Opening hours, event planning, tenant mix, stakeholder reporting, micro-location review and centre strategy. | Continuous or interval footfall by time, entrance or zone. | Temporal pattern reporting and centre-use narratives. | Shopping centres / real estate; Public locations; Travel | What kind of usage pattern does this location really have? | We show when and how people use the location so planning starts from observed behaviour. |
| Catchment distance | How far people appear to move on foot around a station or travel location. | It challenges one-size-fits-all station catchment assumptions. | Station-area planning, access, wayfinding, service placement and location comparison. | Approved movement data, station or terminal boundaries, distance method and privacy review. | Source-informed catchment and distance framing where the approved data and product scope support it. | Travel; Public locations; Shopping centres / real estate | Are we planning from a generic radius, or from how this location is actually used? | We can frame the catchment question from aggregate movement evidence, then validate the method before making recommendations. |
| Distance distribution | How movement is spread across near and farther distance bands. | It shows whether a similar average hides different movement patterns. | Catchment review, route planning, before/after review and stakeholder discussion. | Approved movement data, distance bands, site boundaries and analysis method. | Distribution-based reporting only when the method is approved for the use case. | Travel; Public locations; Shopping centres / real estate | Do two stations with similar averages actually behave the same? | We look beyond the average to the shape of movement, while keeping conclusions review-led. |
| Visit frequency | How often people return or visit within a period. | It helps distinguish one-off visits from repeat-use patterns. | Loyalty, repeat-use, local engagement, catchment quality and campaign review. | Approved visit data aggregated by location and period. | Repeat-visit context where privacy and methodology are approved. | Retail; Shopping centres / real estate; Public locations; QSR | Are we attracting repeat users or mostly one-off visits? | We show whether movement is recurring, occasional or changing over time. |
| Origin-destination flow | Aggregate movement between origins, destinations, routes or zones. | It explains where people come from and where they go next. | Catchment, route planning, transport, access, wayfinding and service placement. | Approved origin/destination or zone movement data, aggregation rules and privacy review. | OD and route-flow framing where approved data supports it. | Travel; Public locations; Shopping centres / real estate; Retail | Where do visitors come from, and which routes do they use? | We frame the movement pattern so planning starts from observed behaviour. |
| Behavioural catchment | The observed visitor-origin area around a location. | It improves on fixed-radius assumptions when actual visitor origins matter. | Site selection, stakeholder reporting, market sizing and location comparison. | Approved aggregate visitor-origin data and catchment method. | Catchment summaries with validation caveats. | Retail; Shopping centres / real estate; Public locations; Travel | Is our real catchment different from the one on paper? | We compare the assumed catchment with aggregate behaviour where the method is approved. |
| Amenity context | How nearby amenities, services or place features relate to movement. | It helps explain why a public place or centre attracts people. | Public-space planning, tenant mix, placemaking and destination strategy. | Approved place-feature data, location boundaries and footfall context. | Movement-plus-place-feature summaries. | Public locations; Shopping centres / real estate | Which amenities or spaces appear to support place vitality? | We connect movement with the place features that may explain it, without claiming causality too early. |
| Place vitality | How active a public place, town centre or shared area is over time. | It gives stakeholders a simple starting point for public-place performance. | Public-location reporting, regeneration, events, intervention review and resilience. | Footfall, dwell, daypart and contextual data by place and period. | Reviewable vitality summaries with caveats. | Public locations; Shopping centres / real estate | Is the place active, recovering or losing momentum? | We turn movement into a stakeholder-ready place-use story. |
| Resilience pattern | Movement before, during and after a disruption or shock. | It shows how a place changes and recovers. | Recovery review, vulnerability analysis and intervention planning. | Historical movement data, event dates and approved context variables. | Comparative trend summaries for human review. | Public locations; Travel | How did this place respond, and has it recovered? | We compare movement patterns over time without claiming cause unless the design supports it. |
| Queue pressure | When a queue, lane, waiting area or service point becomes constrained. | It helps identify when service demand may exceed capacity. | QSR, travel and service planning. | Approved queue, occupancy, service-point or movement data. | Source-informed pressure framing where product scope supports it. | QSR; Travel | When and where does service pressure build? | We show the pressure pattern first, then validate what action is possible. |
| Service-point flow | Demand by lane, counter, entrance, kiosk or service area. | It separates total demand from where service actually happens. | QSR format, staffing, service layout and operational review. | Approved zone, lane or counter definitions and movement/service data. | Service-point summaries where technically feasible. | QSR; Travel | Which service points carry the load? | We compare demand across service points and dayparts. |
| Utilisation pattern | How shared spaces are used across day and week. | It supports aggregate space planning without individual tracking. | Cleaning, facilities, amenities, layout and stakeholder reporting. | Approved occupancy or movement data by shared space and time. | Assumption-led until dedicated smart-building proof is approved. | Smart buildings | Are shared spaces being used as intended? | We can frame the utilisation question, but smart-building proof still needs validation. |
| Data veracity | Confidence that movement data is accurate, stable, comparable and fit for purpose. | It prevents weak data from becoming a strong claim. | Governance, proof quality, reporting and decision readiness. | Source description, validation, benchmarks, bias checks and comparability review. | Governance checklist and caveat language. | All verticals | Can we trust this metric for this decision? | We document what the metric measures, how it was validated and what remains uncertain. |
| Rent and turnover context | Rent, cost or turnover discussion read next to movement and exposure. | It keeps tenant, leasing and rent conversations grounded in approved commercial data rather than footfall alone. | Rent review, relocation, expansion, tenant mix, leasing and asset-performance review. | Customer-approved rent, sales, cost or turnover data plus movement context. | Commercial context only when the customer supplies approved data. | Shopping centres / real estate | Can we support tenant or rent discussions with better evidence? | We can add movement and exposure context, but tenant economics stay conditional on approved customer data. |
| Dashboarding / reporting | A trusted view of KPIs, trends and comparisons. | It makes insight usable in meetings and repeat decisions. | Store reviews, owner/investor reporting, field coaching, operations and sales follow-up. | Clean count, location, time, metadata and approved commercial/contextual data. | Dashboards, reports, trend views and decision-ready summaries. | All verticals | Can we make the next decision from one trusted view? | We turn movement data into a short, repeatable story that sales, managers and stakeholders can use. |

## Non-Retail Deep-Dive KPI Addendum

The current prototype uses these KPI families in the recovered non-retail challenge panels:

| Segment | Primary KPI families | Supporting KPI families | What PFM can deliver in the journey | Caveat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shopping Centres / Real Estate | Centre benchmarking; zone performance; tenant revenue context; campaign impact | Entrance counts; visitor flow; dwell time; footfall signatures; rent and turnover context | Asset, tenant, leasing and owner-reporting summaries built from approved movement and context data. | Tenant economics, rent, turnover and valuation are not inferred by PFM unless supplied and approved. |
| Travel | Catchment distance; visitor flow; travel retail exposure; peak hours; before/after movement | Distance distribution; entrance counts; zone performance; dwell time; occupancy | Validation-led catchment, route, zone, dwell, pressure and travel-retail exposure discovery. | External transport research informs questions only; no safety, security or passenger-outcome claims. |
| Public Locations | Place vitality; amenity context; before/after movement; occupancy; behavioural catchment; dashboarding | Footfall trend; resilience pattern; visitor flow; data veracity; origin-destination flow | Aggregate place-use, intervention-review and accountability summaries with confidence labels. | Public-health, safety, social-impact, regeneration and economic claims need separate proof. |
| Quick Service Restaurants | Queue pressure; service-point flow; peak hours; campaign impact; catchment distance | Occupancy; daypart shift; footfall; conversion rate; visit frequency; peak dining flow | Source-informed service-pressure, daypart, campaign and format/site-fit discovery. | No queue reduction, throughput, labour savings, AI ordering, POS integration or ROI claim. |
| Smart Buildings | Utilisation pattern; occupancy; data veracity; before/after movement; dashboarding | Zone performance; dwell time; peak hours | Assumption-led aggregate space-use, facilities and privacy-safe reporting discovery. | Dedicated smart-building proof and workplace privacy wording are still required. |
