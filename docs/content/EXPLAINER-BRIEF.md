## What an explainer is, and where it appears

Open any scene, click **How does this work?**, and the first tab — **How it
works** — starts with one illustration: the measurement principle, drawn in the
kind of place the question is about. Below it sits one short paragraph saying
what the picture shows.

It is not a product photo — those sit on the **Technology** tab. And it is not
evidence: it shows *how* something is measured, never *what was measured*.

Three are already approved and live, and they set the style:

## Why these six are missing

A capability's artwork is tied to its segment. Counting at a door in a retail
street is not counting at a door in an open-air outlet village, and a Retail
image shown in an Outlet scene would be a picture of the wrong place. So where
no picture of *this* segment exists, the drawer shows text and video only. That
is honest, but thin. These six close the gap.

## The six

Each serves several scenes, so six images cover thirteen.

### 1. Shopping Centre — counting at a threshold

- **Shows:** a mall threshold seen slightly from above — a store entrance off
  the concourse, or the top of an escalator between floors. A soft cone of
  light comes down from a small ceiling sensor and covers the full width of
  the threshold. People walk through it in both directions.
- **Explains:** people are counted as they cross — which is a visit, not a
  passer-by.
- **Used in:** How do visitors move across floors · Which stores are actually
  visited · How many visitors enter the asset
- **File:** `shopping-centre-threshold-counting-explainer.png`

### 2. Retail Park — counting at a unit entrance

- **Shows:** an open-air retail park, a unit's glass entrance with the car
  park behind. The same kind of cone from a sensor above the door, people
  going in and out.
- **Explains:** accurate counting exactly at the entrances you choose to
  measure.
- **Used in:** Which units are visited · How does exposure vary across units
- **File:** `retail-park-unit-entrance-counting-explainer.png`

### 3. Retail Park — re-identification between units

- **Shows:** two views side by side — the entrance of one unit, and the
  entrance of another unit across the park. The same person, seen from
  behind, framed in both views.
- **Explains:** the same observed appearance matched between two separate
  views — so movement from unit to unit and time on site can be measured,
  without learning who anyone is.
- **Used in:** How do visitors move from unit to unit · How long do visitors
  spend in the park
- **File:** `retail-park-anonymous-re-id-explainer.png`

### 4. Outlet Centre — counting at an entrance or store door

- **Shows:** the outlet village's main entrance gateway, or a store door on
  an outlet street. A cone from an overhead sensor across the threshold,
  people passing through.
- **Explains:** real, accurate counting at the entrances and at each store.
- **Used in:** How many visitors enter the outlet streets · Which brands are
  visited
- **File:** `outlet-centre-entrance-counting-explainer.png`

### 5. Outlet Centre — counting lines between zones

- **Shows:** an outlet street seen from a raised angle, with two or three thin
  glowing lines drawn across the paving where one street or zone meets the
  next. Small dome cameras on the buildings. People crossing the lines.
- **Explains:** visitors counted as they cross configured lines between
  streets, zones and anchors — inside the covered views only.
- **Used in:** How do visitors move across streets, zones and anchors · Which
  zones and brand areas receive attention
- **Note:** this one specifically replaces LiDAR and 3D-tracking imagery,
  which is not how an open-air outlet is measured. No point clouds, no
  scanning beams.
- **File:** `outlet-centre-zone-counting-lines-explainer.png`

### 6. Outlet Centre — re-identification between stores

- **Shows:** two views side by side — two different store fronts in the
  outlet village. The same person, from behind, framed in both.
- **Explains:** the same observed appearance matched at the next store — so
  brand-to-brand movement and time in the outlet can be measured.
- **Used in:** How do visitors move from one brand to another · How long do
  visitors stay in the outlet
- **File:** `outlet-centre-anonymous-re-id-explainer.png`

## The rules every image has to keep

These are not taste. Each one is enforced by a test, or protects a claim the
product is not allowed to make.

- **No faces.** People seen from behind or from a distance. No face framed,
  outlined or highlighted.
- **No text, numbers or labels burnt in.** No counts, percentages, times,
  names or UI panels. A number in a picture is read as a result.
- **No real brand names or logos** — not on shopfronts, bags or cars. Blank
  or fictional fascia only. (Two of the earlier outlet candidates were
  rejected for exactly this.)
- **Its own kind of place.** A retail-park image is a retail park. Never a
  shopping-centre image reused with a different overlay — that was caught once
  already, with a mean pixel difference of 1.9.
- **No hardware claims.** A small generic sensor or dome camera is fine; a
  recognisable product is not. The explainer shows a principle, not a device.
- **No completeness.** Coverage is partial. Show gaps between camera views
  rather than a seamless field over everything.
